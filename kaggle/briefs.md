# Twenty briefs

Drafts for the benchmark. Both models receive the same list. No company is named. Each brief states the role, the seniority, and the topics the ad must address. It does not supply the facts. How concrete those facts are is left to the model.

Shared instruction, sent with every brief: write one English job ad for an unnamed employer. Do not invent a company name. Address every topic below.

## 1. Backend engineer

- **Seniority:** junior
- **Address:** the service they will work on; languages and data stores; who reviews their code; team size; where the work is done; pay; what the first six months look like

## 2. Frontend engineer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** the product surface they own; framework and styling approach; how design and engineering split work; release cadence; office or remote expectation; pay; years and kind of experience asked for

## 3. iOS engineer

- **Seniority:** senior
- **Address:** which app and which platform; UI toolkit; release process to the store; on-call or release-duty expectation; team around them; pay; the hardest technical requirement

## 4. Android engineer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** the app area they own; language and UI toolkit; device and OS versions they must support; collaboration with iOS; location; pay; experience asked for

## 5. Full-stack engineer

- **Seniority:** senior
- **Address:** what they are expected to ship across UI and API; the stack; how large the team is; decision rights versus a tech lead; hybrid or remote pattern; pay; on-call

## 6. Data engineer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** the pipelines or warehouse they own; tools; who consumes the data; freshness or latency expectation; team size; location; pay; experience asked for

## 7. Analytics engineer

- **Seniority:** junior
- **Address:** what they model and for whom; warehouse and transformation tool; how metrics are reviewed; mentorship; office expectation; pay; the skills the ad treats as required versus taught

## 8. Data scientist

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** the decisions their work is meant to change; whether the work is offline analysis, production models, or both; tools; stakeholders; location; pay; experience asked for

## 9. Machine learning engineer

- **Seniority:** senior
- **Address:** what is already in production; training and serving stack; how models are evaluated before release; on-call for model or pipeline failure; team; pay; the experience that is actually required

## 10. Site reliability engineer

- **Seniority:** senior
- **Address:** which systems they are on the hook for; cloud and observability tools; on-call rotation and what a typical page looks like; error-budget or uptime expectation; team size; pay; where they sit relative to product engineering

## 11. Platform engineer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** what internal platform they build; who the users are (other engineers); technologies; support rotation; how requests are prioritized; location; pay

## 12. Security engineer

- **Seniority:** staff
- **Address:** whether the work is product security, detection, or both; scope across teams; tools and practices they set; incident duty; travel or on-site expectation; pay; what "staff" means for decision rights

## 13. Security analyst

- **Seniority:** junior
- **Address:** what queue or alert stream they work; tools; shift or follow-the-sun expectation; who signs off on escalations; training in the first months; location; pay

## 14. QA engineer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** what product they test; the split between manual and automated work; automation stack; where they sit in the release path; team; location; pay; experience asked for

## 15. Engineering manager

- **Seniority:** manager
- **Address:** how many people they manage and at what levels; what the team builds; how much of the week is hiring, people management, and technical work; what they still do hands-on, if anything; location; pay; the experience the ad requires of a first-time versus an experienced manager

## 16. Tech lead

- **Seniority:** lead
- **Address:** whether this is a people-management role or an individual-contributor lead; the system they are responsible for; time in design review versus coding; team size; on-call; pay; location

## 17. Principal engineer

- **Seniority:** principal
- **Address:** the technical area they set direction for; how many teams that covers; what they still build themselves; how decisions are made with engineering managers; travel; pay; what the ad treats as evidence of principal-level work

## 18. Software engineering intern

- **Seniority:** intern
- **Address:** duration and dates; the team and the kind of project; mentor arrangement; return-offer practice; pay; location and whether housing or relocation is mentioned; what the intern is expected to have already done

## 19. Technical writer

- **Seniority:** mid
- **Address:** who reads the docs; what they own (API reference, tutorials, or internal runbooks); tools and review process; how they work with engineers; location; pay; experience asked for

## 20. Production support engineer

- **Seniority:** junior
- **Address:** what they handle during a shift; ticket or page volume the ad is willing to describe; tools; escalation path; schedule including nights or weekends; training; pay; location
